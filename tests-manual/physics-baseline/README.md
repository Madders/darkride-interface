# Physics baseline (PHYSICS.md round 0)

How well does the client, as it stands, keep an avatar on a moving platform? This test gives the number every later round has to beat.

`baseline.js` creates a cabin (a 4 × 8 m floor with low walls) in the serverless `world.json`, puts the avatar on it, and runs five phases of about ten seconds each:

| Phase | The cabin | The avatar |
|---|---|---|
| straight-unparented | velocity 5 m/s, straight | standing, not parented |
| straight-parented | velocity 5 m/s, straight | standing, parented to the cabin |
| curve-unparented | position and rotation set every frame around a 15 m circle at 5 m/s | standing, not parented |
| curve-parented | the same circle | standing, parented |
| walk-parented | velocity 5 m/s, straight | parented, walking across the cabin and back with the scripted motor |

Every script frame it records the cabin's transform, the avatar's world position, the avatar's position in the cabin's frame (computed), and, when parented, the engine's own stored local position (the value other clients receive). The summary reports, per phase:

- **drift**: how far the avatar moved in the cabin's frame from the start of the phase to its end. Standing still should give zero. This is the slide.
- **jitter**: the frame-to-frame change of the avatar's local position with the phase's mean change removed, as rms and maximum, horizontal and vertical. Standing still should give zero. This is what the rider sees and what other clients receive.

**Frame mode (PHYSICS.md round 2):** `run.sh <app> <results dir> frame` runs `baseline-frame.js`, which makes the cabin a moving reference frame (userData `{"frame": true}`). The engine then parents the avatar to the cabin itself whenever the avatar stands in the cabin's volume, so the "unparented" phases measure that automatic membership, the summary adds how many frames of each phase the avatar was parented and the world-position step at every parent change, and a last phase, **step-off**, rides for two seconds and then walks off the open back end of the moving cabin onto the ground. Expected on round 2: zero drift and zero stored-local jitter in every riding phase, one crossing out in step-off with a 0.5 m drop and no re-entry.

Run it from a shell, serverless:

```sh
D=$(cd "$(dirname "$0")" && pwd)   # this directory
R=/tmp/physics-baseline && mkdir -p "$R"
.../Darkride.app/Contents/MacOS/Darkride --url "file://$D/world.json" --testScript "$D/baseline.js" --testResultsLocation "$R" 2>&1 | tee "$R/client.log"
grep PHYSICS-BASELINE "$R/client.log"
```

The samples are in `$R/physics-baseline.json`. The client quits by itself when the schedule ends (about 80 s).

Caveat: the computed local position is made from two reads a moment apart (the cabin's transform and the avatar's position), so it carries up to one frame of the cabin's motion as noise (about 6 mm rms, 83 mm max at 5 m/s and 60 Hz); the stored local position has no such noise and is the number to read whenever it is printed.

**Two clients (PHYSICS.md round 3):** `run-pair.sh <app> <results dir> [udp port]` runs the frame baseline on a domain with a second client watching. Start a sandbox domain from the same build first (`~/git/tivolicloud/domain-sandbox.sh physics <build dir> 42102` in the docs repo); the mover creates the ground, the light and a station platform behind the cabin when the world lacks them, and gives every entity a lifetime. The observer (`observer.js`) steps onto the stopped cabin from the platform, rides every phase, steps off behind the cabin when the mover does and watches it go from the ground. It reads the phase from the cabin's description and prints `PHYSICS-OBSERVER` lines, per phase:

- its own stored local position's jitter while riding (the engine's value, no read race);
- the other rider's stored local position as the avatar mixer delivers it (`AvatarList.getAvatar(id).localPosition`, added for this);
- the other rider relative to itself in the cabin's frame (what a rider sees of the other; two reads a moment apart);
- the cabin's own world step per frame on this non-owning client, while riding and from the ground: the evenness of the glide;
- its crossings, with the world-position step at each.

The samples are in `<results dir>/observer/physics-observer.json` and the mover's in `<results dir>/mover/physics-baseline.json`.

Results so far are in `results/`: `2026-10-12-bullet.json` (round 0), `2026-10-12-jolt.json` (round 1), `2026-10-12-jolt-frame.json` (round 2, frame mode) and `2026-10-12-jolt-round2-plain.json` (round 2 build, plain mode).

**On a track (PHYSICS.md round 4):** `run.sh <app> <results dir> track` runs `track.js`: a closed, stadium-shaped `Track` entity (straights of 40 m, half circles of 15 m, a little bank at the apex of each curve) with two stations ten seconds each, and the cabin as a frame that rides it (`frame: true`, `trackID`), moved by the engine's track follower instead of a script. The avatar steps on from the platform while the cabin stands at the station, rides a lap and steps off onto the platform when the cabin is back. The summary (`PHYSICS-TRACK` lines) is per phase of the ride as read off the cabin's own motion (standing, accelerating, cruising, curve, braking), with the same drift and jitter numbers as the other modes, the cabin's own world step per frame, and how far from the track point the cabin stood at the far station. Samples in `<results dir>/physics-track.json`. `run-pair.sh <app> <results dir> <port> track` runs it as the mover on a domain with `observer.js` on the second client, which boards at the station and steps off when the mover does, as in round 3.

**The physics server (PHYSICS.md round 5):** it is off by default in a domain's settings; `run-monorail.sh`, `run-airship.sh` and `run-aircraft.sh` turn it on in the physics sandbox's `domain-config.json` before they start it, and `run-pair.sh` and `run-rover.sh` need a sandbox started that way. `run-monorail.sh <app> <results dir> [udp port] [riders] [walkers] [loss]` is the acceptance test of the whole job. It restarts the physics sandbox from the app's build (`domain-sandbox.sh`, whose assignment-client monitor now also runs the physics server), with `bots/monorail-bot.js` as the domain's persistent scripts: twenty agents that board the cabin and sit (their parent and local position go through the avatar mixer as a real rider's do) and twenty that walk a circle on the ground below. Then `observer.js` runs under `TIVOLI_PACKET_LOSS=0.05` (five per cent of its datagrams dropped, in and out; a test knob in the UDP socket, off unless set) and `track.js` makes the track and the cabin and rides a lap. The server takes the cabin from its creator at simulation priority 255 (`track.js` logs the owner whenever it changes, with the train state the server publishes), drives the train and publishes the train's state on the Track entity; both clients run the same follower on from it, so neither is the owner any more and the cabin's glide on each is the number to read: `cabin world step` (its mean, its rms deviation and how many steps are over twice the mean, which are corrections landing). The observer also reports the crowd (avatars seen, riders aboard) and the riders' stored local positions in aggregate. Before the start the harness stops the sandbox and strips every `baseline-*` entity of an earlier run from its world file (their lifetime is 600 s, and the server would resume their train); the mover also deletes what it can see, and the observer and the bots board the newest cabin made since they started and keep it. The sandbox's logs of the run are copied beside the results (the physics server's lines are in the assignment-client log, under `[physics-server]`). At the end the bots are taken out of the sandbox's settings and it is restarted clean. Bots only while Auxi's mind is off, and never in the main world.

**The Land Rover on the terrain (PHYSICS.md round 6):** `run-rover.sh <app> <results dir> [udp port|serverless] [loss] [delay_ms]` drives a wheeled vehicle over a scripted course. `make-terrain.py` writes the course's ground into the results dir (192 m square, 32 m tall, 0.5 m voxels: rolling hills with a flat pad in the middle, in the terrain library's file format), the results dir is served over HTTP on port+78 so the physics server and the clients fetch the same file, and `landrover.js` (the Land Rover Series III 88" as entities: a Box chassis with the `vehicle` property group from its spec sheet, four Cylinder wheels as children with `vehicle.type "wheel"`) and `rover.js` are copied beside it, one self-contained script per role. The driver's client makes the terrain, stands beside the pad (the physics only simulates what is near the avatar), waits for the ground (a ray cast), makes the car, sits in the driver's seat as `vehicles.js` would (parented to the chassis at the seat, collisions off) and runs the course through `Entities.setVehicleControls`: idle 3 s, full throttle 6 s, brake, reverse, hand brake, a loop to the right and one to the left at half throttle, a slalom, a stop; then it stands up. On a domain the passenger's client starts first, finds the newest car and sits in the passenger seat; the driver may run under `TIVOLI_PACKET_LOSS` and `TIVOLI_PACKET_DELAY_MS` (a hold on the socket's outgoing datagrams). Every script frame both record the chassis's pose and velocities, the speed along its forward, the authority's published state (the input sequence it reached, engine rpm, gear, the state's domain time), the local simulation's live telemetry (`Entities.getVehicleStats().live`: engine rpm, gear, clutch, wheel rpm, slip, suspension, contact) and statistics (corrections, substeps replayed, inputs sent and received), and their own stored local position against the seat. The summary (`PHYSICS-ROVER` lines) is per phase of the course: speed along forward, distance, heading change, gears, tilt, the seat error and the stored local position's jitter and drift (the rider must not slide), the chassis's world step per frame (its evenness), corrections and replays; the passenger adds the driver's stored local position as the mixer delivers it. Samples in `<results dir>/driver/physics-rover.json` and `.../passenger/physics-rover.json`; the sandbox's assignment-client log of the run is copied beside them. Serverless is the fallback path (one client, the authority); on the sandbox the physics server takes the car at priority 255, the driver predicts and reconciles, the passenger follows.

**Drive it yourself:** `drive-rover.sh [app] [udp port|serverless]` opens a client on the physics sandbox with the terrain and the Land Rover made for you (`rover-playground.js`); click the car to get in, W/S or the up and down arrows for throttle and brake, A/D or the left and right arrows to steer, Space for the hand brake, F to get out, R for a fresh car on the pad, T back to the pad.

**The airship (PHYSICS.md round 7):** `run-airship.sh <app> <results dir> [udp port|serverless] [loss] [delay_ms]` flies a lighter-than-air vehicle over the same terrain. `airship-rig.js` is the ship as entities: a Box deck that is the hull the physics flies (the `vehicle` group with `type "airship"`: a 1,500 m³ helium envelope, 4 kN of propellers, a 3 kN vertical thruster, 12 m² fins 17 m aft, 250 kg of ballast, so it sits 30 kg heavy on the ground) and a moving reference frame (`frame true`) whose top is the deck; an envelope and fins for the look (collisionless children), rails and a helm post that stand in the ship's own world. The pilot's client makes the terrain, the ship on the pad, stands at the helm (a member of the ship's frame by where it stands, as `vehicles.js` does it: no parenting by hand) and flies a scripted course through `Entities.setVehicleControls`: moored, ballast dropped, lift-off on the vertical thruster, a climb to 35 m, a cruise, a turn each way under full rudder, the nose up and down on the elevator, the way back to the pad under an autopilot (a heading and a height), gas vented, a descent, a landing. On a domain the crew's client starts first: it finds the ship, climbs aboard at the stern, drops a ball on the deck, and through the flight stands and walks fore and aft by turns (the rails keep it aboard); after the landing it moves the ship with one explicit edit of its position, which the physics server adopts as a teleport, and reports that it came along with the ship, its local position unchanged. Both record the hull's pose and velocities, the live telemetry (altitude, air density, buoyancy, ballast, fill, thrust, airspeed, sideslip), the published state, the vehicle statistics, their membership of the frame and their stored local position; the crew adds the ball's local position and the pilot's stored local position as the mixer delivers it. The summary (`PHYSICS-AIRSHIP` lines) is per phase of the course: height, climb rate, speed and airspeed, heading change, pitch and roll, the lift and the ballast, the stored local position's jitter and drift standing and walking (a crew member standing on the deck must not move in the ship's frame while it flies), the ball on the deck, the hull's world step per frame, corrections and replays. Samples in `<results dir>/pilot/physics-airship.json` and `.../crew/physics-airship.json`; the runs of 10-13 are in `results/2026-10-13-round7-airship-*.json.gz`, gzipped (28 to 39 MB of JSON each). On a domain the script first restarts the physics sandbox from the client's build, with the earlier runs' `rover-terrain` and `airship-*` entities taken out of its world (they live 600 s). Serverless is the fallback path (the pilot alone, the authority); on the sandbox the physics server takes the ship at priority 255, the pilot predicts and reconciles, the crew's client follows.

**Fly it yourself:** `fly-airship.sh [app] [udp port|serverless]` opens a client on the physics sandbox with the terrain and the ship made for you (`airship-playground.js`); walk aboard at the stern and click the ship to take the helm: W/S set the throttle (X cuts it), A/D or left/right the rudder, up/down the elevator, Space and C the vertical thruster up and down, B drops ballast, V vents gas, F leaves the helm; R for a fresh ship on the pad, T back to the pad.
